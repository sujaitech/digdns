Check DNS propagation

Where to check from

Build an SPF record

Pick the services that send email for your domain. The record and its DNS lookup count update as you go, using the same check as the domain report.

Explain an email’s headers or a bounce

Paste a message’s headers to see who really sent it, whether it passed SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and where it was delayed. Or paste a bounce (“Undelivered Mail Returned to Sender”) to find out why it failed and how to fix it. It’s all worked out in your browser: nothing you paste is sent anywhere.

Test a real email

Get a one-time address, send it an email from the account or app you want to check, and see how it scores: authentication, the sending server’s reputation, a SpamAssassin spam score and the content.

See a website from around the world

Open a site in a real browser from another country to see what people there get: the page itself, how long it takes, and whether they’re blocked or sent somewhere else.

Look up an IP address

See your own public IP, or check any address: who runs it, whether it’s reported for abuse, on spam blocklists or a Tor exit.

Test your internet speed

Measures download, upload and latency from this device to Cloudflare’s nearest data centre, including how much your connection slows down when it’s busy.

Build a DMARC record

Choose how receivers should treat mail that fails SPF and DKIM, and where to send reports. Start with monitoring, then tighten.

What the report covers

Website. Whether it’s up, how fast it answers, its redirects and security headers, and what it’s built with.

Registration and hosting. Registrar, expiry and locks, who hosts the site, and who handles its email.

DNS and email authentication. Every common record, nameserver health, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC with builders to fix them.

Mail servers, blocklists and SSL. MX reverse DNS, MTA-STS, whether your servers’ IPs are on spam blocklists, plus certificate expiry and allowed issuers.

When to use this

After you change a DNS record, resolvers keep serving their cached copy until its TTL runs out. This shows which ones already have the new answer, and how long the rest may hold the old one.

        How it works
        
        
        
          
          
            
        1. Get a test address. It works for one hour.
          2. 
            
        2. Send it a normal email from the mailbox, website form or app you want to check, written the way you’d write to a customer.
          3. 
            
        3. Keep this page open. The report appears within a minute of the email arriving.
          4. 
          
        
          
          
        The email is kept for one hour, then deleted. To score it, its contents are sent to Postmark’s free SpamAssassin service. This can’t tell you whether Gmail or Outlook will put it in the inbox; nobody can without real test mailboxes there.

        Enter a domain above. Every place is checked at once (does the home page answer, how fast, which server), then you can open the site in a real browser from any place to see it.
        
      
        
      
        
      
        
      
        The coloured marks come from one request to the home page from a test machine in each country (Globalping), refreshed every 10 minutes. An answer of HTTP 200 means the server responded, not that the page looks right: open a place to see it. Opening a place uses a real browser that reaches the internet through an address in that country (Browserless’s proxies); the exact city can vary. Opened views are kept for 6 hours, and new ones are limited each day because every page load is paid for by the megabyte.

    VdigDNS is open source. To see how it’s used, each check is logged with the domain or IP checked, your IP address, approximate location and browser, and deleted after 30 days. The IP lookup shows your IP using ipify and checks addresses with AbuseIPDB and the Tor Project’s exit list; the speed test runs against Cloudflare. World view’s status checks run on Globalping’s free test machines, and its visits from other countries, like website screenshots, are taken by Browserless; email tests are kept for one hour and scored with Postmark’s SpamAssassin service.
